==**AWS Organizations solves the complex administrative and financial challenge of managing, billing, and securing multiple distinct AWS accounts at scale under a unified corporate hierarchy**==**.** You can configure these multi-account rules through the [AWS Organizations Console](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/organizations/) or reference the Official AWS Organizations Documentation. [[1](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/industries/best-practices-for-aws-organizations-service-control-policies-in-a-multi-account-environment/), [2](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/mt/simplified-multi-account-governance-with-aws-organizations-all-features/)]

How AWS Organizations Solves Multi-Account Challenges

- **Account Sprawl and Isolation:** Solves the problem of running every single application inside one chaotic AWS account. Creating independent accounts for different teams or lifecycles (like development, test, and production) builds strict security boundaries and limits the impact of a system failure. [[1](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/mt/best-practices-for-organizational-units-with-aws-organizations/), [2](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/mt/multi-account-strategy-for-small-and-medium-businesses/)]
- **Consolidated Billing:** Solves the problem of receiving dozens of separate invoices from AWS. It combines all charges from every member account into a single monthly bill and automatically pools usage to qualify for volume pricing discounts and shared savings plans. [[1](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/organizations/latest/userguide/orgs_introduction.html), [2](https://aws.amazon.com/organizations/customers/)]
- **Organizational Units (OUs):** Solve the problem of applying governance to random lists of servers. OUs let you arrange your accounts into logical, tree-like hierarchies (such as a _Security OU_ or a _Workloads OU_) so you can manage rules for an entire group at once.
    
    [[1](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/organizations/latest/userguide/orgs_introduction.html), [2](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/mt/multi-account-strategy-for-small-and-medium-businesses/)]
- **Service Control Policies (SCPs):** Solve the problem of local administrators overriding corporate safety rules. SCPs act as top-down permission guardrails that restrict the maximum available actions for any user or role across an entire branch of your organizational tree. [[1](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/organizations/latest/userguide/orgs_introduction.html), [2](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/industries/best-practices-for-aws-organizations-service-control-policies-in-a-multi-account-environment/)]
- **Centralized Security Integration:** Solves the problem of security blind spots across distributed teams. It allows you to auto-enable and delegate management for foundational services—like [Amazon GuardDuty](https://aws.amazon.com/guardduty/) and [AWS Config](https://aws.amazon.com/config/)—across all member accounts from a single central location.
